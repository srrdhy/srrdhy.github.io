2. Implementing the Inverse Discrete Fourier Transform in Python

In the previous article, we implemented the discrete Fourier transform (DFT) in Python.

In this article, let’s look at the inverse process: converting a vector of frequency values into a vector of time values.

Mathematically, the inverse discrete Fourier transform (IDFT) is given by:

$$x[n]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X[k]\cdot e^{i2\pi\frac{k}{N}n}$$
where:

  • $N$ is the number of samples
  • $n$ is the index of the current sample
  • $k$ is the current frequency
  • $X[k]$ is the DFT at $n$
  • $x[n]$ is the iDFT at $n$

Since the matrix of the discrete Fourier transform is square and invertible, the inverse transform is feasible. Ideally, we should be able to recover our original noisy signal.

The DFT function returned a vector of complex numbers, containing the magnitude and phase at each frequency. We call this vector X_K.
