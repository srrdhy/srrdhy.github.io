DB-PINN

Now look at the part inside the red box in the figure above: once the difficulty coefficient is incorporated, GW-PINN transitions into DB-PINN, which blends inter-balancing and intra-balancing.

Similar to method 1 in GW-PINN, which uses max and mean, take DB_PINN_mean in DB-PINN as an example. First perform the equivalent of GW-PINN, computing for each constraint term $i \in \{ \text{bc}, \text{ic}, \text{d} \}$ the ratio:
$$ \frac{S_r}{S_i} = \frac{\max |\nabla L_r|}{\operatorname{mean} |\lambda_i \nabla L_i|} $$
Then sum them, $\sum \frac{S_r}{S_i}=G$, to obtain the total scale, which is named hat_all .

Intuition: if the constraint terms are overall much “weaker” than the PDE residual, hat_all will be large, and the aggregate weight of the three constraint terms is raised as a whole, aligning the global scale first.

Now we begin to distribute the total scale over the internal terms.

First, a brief introduction to the historical average: given a sequence of vectors $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_t$, whenever a new $x_t$ arrives, update the “arithmetic mean so far”:
$$\mu_t = \frac{1}{t} \sum_{k=1}^{t} x_k$$
Recomputing directly from the definition would require storing the entire history. The equivalent online recursion is:
$$\mu_t = \mu_{t-1} + \frac{1}{t}(x_t - \mu_{t-1})=\left(1 - \frac{1}{t}\right)\mu_{t-1} + \frac{1}{t} x_t$$

Now we introduce the difficulty index $I$, where $k$ is the $k$-th weight update (updated once every mm steps):
$$I^{(k)} = \frac{L^{(k)}}{\mu^{(k)}}$$
Note that this is an element-wise/component-wise division, where:
$$L^{(k)} = \left[ L_r^{(k)}, L_{bc}^{(k)}, L_{ic}^{(k)}, L_d^{(k)} \right]$$
These are the four atomic losses at the current $k$-th weight update
$$\mu^{(k)} = \left[ \mu_r^{(k)}, \mu_{bc}^{(k)}, \mu_{ic}^{(k)}, \mu_d^{(k)} \right]$$
This is the historical mean up to the $k$-th weight update, i.e. the mean of $L^{(k)}$; referring to the formula for $\mu_t$, we have
$$\mu^{(k)} = \left(1 - \frac{1}{k}\right)\mu^{(k-1)} + \frac{1}{k} L^{(k)}$$
Note that this applies to each atomic loss term: every weight update brings in a new $L^{(k)}$, which is combined with the previous $L^{(k)}$ to compute the mean. In the code, the historical mean is running_mean_L; k is N_l, initialized to 0 and incremented by 1 at each update; L_t = torch.stack((l_reg, l_bc, l_ic, l_data)), i.e. the current $L^{(k)}$, and the difficulty index is l_t_vector = L_t/running_mean_L, which corresponds exactly to $I^{(k)} = \frac{L^{(k)}}{\mu^{(k)}}$.

At this point we must distribute the total scale $G$, i.e. hat_all, among the 3 constraint terms, according to the difficulty coefficients:
$$I = \left[ \frac{L_{r}}{\mu_{r}}, \frac{L_{bc}}{\mu_{bc}}, \frac{L_{ic}}{\mu_{ic}}, \frac{L_d}{\mu_d} \right]$$
Whichever term has a current loss larger relative to its historical mean has a larger $I$, which means it is harder to train. Note that l_t_vector[0] is $\frac{L_{r}}{\mu_{r}}$; the residual does not take part in the allocation of $G$, so:

$$ \hat{\lambda}_{bc} = G \cdot \frac{I_{bc}}{I_{bc} + I_{ic} + I_d}, \quad \hat{\lambda}_{ic} = G \cdot \frac{I_{ic}}{I_{bc} + I_{ic} + I_d}, \quad \hat{\lambda}_d = G \cdot \frac{I_d}{I_{bc} + I_{ic} + I_d}. $$

These $\hat{\lambda}_{bc}$ and the others are, in effect, instantaneous suggested weights.

Finally, the weight coefficients are updated online with a hyperparameter-free, robust smoothing rule:
$$\lambda_i \leftarrow \left(1 - \frac{1}{N_l}\right)\lambda_i + \frac{1}{N_l} \hat{\lambda}_i$$
As the number of weight updates grows, $\frac{1}{N_l}$ becomes smaller at later stages, so the update becomes smoother. Compare this with the EMA update in GW-PINN:
$$ \lambda_i \leftarrow (1 - \alpha)\lambda_i + \alpha \cdot \frac{s_r}{s_i} $$
$\frac{1}{N_l}$ replaces $\alpha$, and $\hat{\lambda}_i$ replaces $\frac{s_r}{s_i}$.

Note that at the very start of training the following values are initialized to 0, so that the historical mean at the first weight update is exactly the current loss.

The DB_PINN_std and DB_PINN_kurt methods are similar; only the computation of hat_all is changed.
