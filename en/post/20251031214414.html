Physics-Informed Neural Networks

The core idea is to build a neural network with parameters $\theta$ that learns an approximate solution $\hat{u}_\theta (\mathbf{x}, t)$ of the PDE

$$ \begin{align*} \mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}, t}[u(\mathbf{x}, t)] &= f(\mathbf{x}, t), \quad \mathbf{x} \in \Omega, \, t \in [0, T] \\ \mathcal{B}_{\mathbf{x}, t}[u(\mathbf{x}, t)] &= g(\mathbf{x}, t), \quad \mathbf{x} \in \partial\Omega, \, t \in [0, T] \\ u(\mathbf{x}, 0) &= h(\mathbf{x}), \quad \mathbf{x} \in \Omega \end{align*} $$

We sample points $\left\{ \left( \mathbf{X}_i^r, t_i^r \right) \right\}_{i=1}^{N_r}$ in the spatio-temporal domain $(\Omega \times [0, T])$ and compute the PDE residual loss:
$$ \mathcal{L}^r(\theta) = \frac{1}{N_r} \sum_{i=1}^{N_r} \left\| \mathcal{N}_{\mathbf{x}, t} \left[ \hat{u}_\theta \left( \mathbf{x}_i^r, t_i^r \right) \right] - f \left( \mathbf{x}_i^r, t_i^r \right) \right\|^2 $$
plus a group of condition losses, such as boundary conditions, initial conditions, and additional data.

During PINN training, the weight coefficients are numbers of enormous influence that are nonetheless hard to tune. Many researchers have sought dynamic weighting methods, and the two main routes today are the learning approach and the calculating approach.

The learning approach treats the weight coefficients as learnable parameters optimized together within the network; the calculating approach takes the perspective of the neural tangent kernel (NTK) and computes the weights from the ratio of gradient magnitudes $\left| \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}^r \right| / \left| \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}^i \right|$. DB-PINN complements GW-PINN, a concrete recipe within the existing calculating approach.

GW-PINN

It aims at inter-balancing, as shown inside the yellow box in the figure above. Prior work has shown that the PDE residual usually dominates among the loss terms, so the weight coefficient of the residual term is now fixed to 1, and the weights of the remaining condition loss terms are adjusted during training.
$$ \mathcal{G} = \sum_{i=1}^{M} \frac{\max\left\{ \left| \nabla_\theta \mathcal{L}^r \right| \right\}}{\left| \nabla_\theta \lambda^i \mathcal{L}^i \right|} $$

The total loss typically consists of four atomic losses: the residual loss $L_r$, the boundary loss $L_{bc}$, the initial loss $L_{ic}$, and the data loss $L_{d}$,
$$ \mathcal{L} = L_r + \lambda_{bc} L_{bc} + \lambda_{ic} L_{ic} + \lambda_d L_d $$
In practice, gradient imbalance frequently arises during training: the back-propagated gradient of one term with respect to the network parameters is far larger than those of the others, so the other terms “learn nothing”.

What GW-PINN does is align the magnitudes of the gradients of the various losses with respect to the network parameters. The recipe is to give each condition term a weight
$$ \lambda_i \propto \frac{s_r}{s_i} $$
where $s_r$ is the “gradient scale of the residual term” and $s_i$ is the gradient scale of the $i$-th condition term ($bc,ic,d$). This way, when a condition term’s gradient is on the small side, its $\lambda_i$ is amplified, and vice versa, so that alignment across classes (inter) is achieved.

Depending on the algorithms for $s_r$ and $s_i$, GW-PINN has 3 different implementations:

method 1, the mean version:
$$ s_r = \max\left| \nabla_\theta L_r \right|, \quad s_i = \operatorname{mean}\left| \nabla_\theta L_i \right| $$
weight update:
$$ \lambda_i \leftarrow (1 - \alpha)\lambda_i + \alpha \cdot \frac{s_r}{s_i} $$
Here EMA (Exponential Moving Average) smoothing is used for the weight update to suppress transient noise; for example, $\alpha$ is taken as 0.1

method 2, the std version:
$$ s_r = std\left( \nabla_\theta L_r \right), \quad s_i = std\left( \nabla_\theta L_i \right) $$
same update as above

method 3, the kurt version:

First construct $\text{cov} = \text{std}/\text{kurt}$, then update the weights:
$$ \lambda_i \leftarrow (1 - \alpha)\lambda_i + \alpha \cdot \frac{\text{cov}_r}{\text{cov}_i} $$

Algorithms for max, mean, std and kurt

Taking method 1 as an example, the “scale” of the residual term is
$$ S_r = \max_{\theta} \left| \nabla_{\theta} L_r \right| $$
that is: the global maximum, over all trainable parameters $\theta$, of the absolute value of the gradient of $L_r$.

The “scale” of a condition term (bc/ic/data) is
$$ S_i = \operatorname{mean}_{\theta} \left| \nabla_\theta (\lambda_i L_i) \right| $$
that is: for each condition term $L_i$, under the current weight $\lambda_i$, the overall mean of the absolute values of the gradients with respect to all parameters. Note that $\lambda_i$ appears here, corresponding to the lambg argument of the loss_grad_max_mean function; since the residual term $L_r$ has a weight of 1 by default, no lambg argument needs to be passed for it.
